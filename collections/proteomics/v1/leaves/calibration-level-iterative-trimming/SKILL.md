# calibration-level-iterative-trimming

## Summary

Iterative determination of a calibration's final linear range by repeatedly removing boundary concentration levels whose precision-based average bias (PB_av) exceeds the target threshold TPB_av, then refitting, until both the lowest (Cl) and highest (Ch) retained levels pass. This is the core algorithm CalibraCurve uses to derive lower and upper limits of quantification.

## When to use

Use when a concentration–response calibration table (replicate measurements per level, e.g. from SRM/MRM/PRM assays) shows non-linearity at the extremes — e.g. reduced product ion intensities at high analyte concentrations — and Cl/Ch limits of quantification must be computed.

## When NOT to use

- The data are not a dilution/concentration series with replicate measurements per level (CalibraCurve targets concentration or dilution series measured by mass spectrometry, usually in replicates)
- The concentration–response relationship is linear across all measured levels, so no boundary trimming is needed
- A single-level quality flag is required rather than a two-sided (lowest and highest) linear-range boundary determination

## Inputs

- Concentration–response calibration table with replicate instrument-response measurements per concentration level (e.g. from SRM/MRM/PRM targeted MS runs)
- Target threshold TPB_av for the acceptable average precision-based bias per level
- Choice of weighted or unweighted linear model fitting

## Outputs

- Final linear range as boundary levels (Cl, Ch)
- Ordered log of removed calibration levels
- linear_range.csv containing the final Cl, Ch values and removed-level log
- Calibration curve and response factor plots generated by CalibraCurve

## How to apply

Load the calibration concentration–response table with replicate measurements per level into R and fit linear (weighted and unweighted) models of instrument response versus concentration using CalibraCurve's lm-based routines. For each measurement compute the precision-based bias (PB) from residuals against the fitted model, then aggregate per calibration level into PB_av (mean bias) and PB_cv (bias variability). Iteratively test the two boundary levels: if the lowest level (Cl) or highest level (Ch) has PB_av above the target threshold TPB_av, remove that boundary level (applying the documented highest/lowest tie-breaking rules), refit the model, and repeat. The iteration terminates when PB_av of both remaining boundary levels is below TPB_av; the final linear range is then (Cl, Ch) and the ordered log of removed levels is written alongside it.

## Evaluation signals

- Termination invariant: at the final iteration, PB_av of both the lowest retained level (Cl) and the highest retained level (Ch) is below TPB_av
- linear_range.csv exists and contains the final Cl and Ch concentration values together with the ordered log of removed levels
- Removed levels must always be boundary levels (lowest or highest) chosen per the documented tie-breaking rules — no interior level is ever removed
- A model refit follows every level removal; the final linear range must be based on a model fitted to the retained (final) level set
- Monotonicity sanity check: the reported linear range must be a subset of the original measured concentration levels, with Cl < Ch

## Limitations

- CalibraCurve assumes a linear relationship between concentration and intensity is restricted to a certain range; data outside assumptions (e.g. saturating detector response beyond the highest level) can only be handled by boundary removal, not by curve reshaping
- The paper's provided discussion section was absent, so explicit limitations and failure modes of the algorithm could not be extracted from the authors
- Quality of the resulting linear range depends on the chosen TPB_av threshold and on replicate quality; CalibraCurve terms of use state results must be validated before further use and are not for clinical diagnosis
