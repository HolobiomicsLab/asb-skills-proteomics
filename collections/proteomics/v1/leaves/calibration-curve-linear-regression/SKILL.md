# calibration-curve-linear-regression

## Summary

Fit linear calibration curves to targeted-MS (SRM/MRM, e.g. Agilent 6490 MRM dilution series) concentration-response data with CalibraCurve, iteratively determine the final linear range via precision-based bias (PB_av/PB_cv) thresholds, and compare unweighted vs 1/x^2-weighted linear models using both R-squared and PB_av trueness.

## When to use

Use when you have replicate-grouped dilution-series intensities for one or more peptides (e.g. transition-level data for WTVFQK/MFAP4) and must determine the linear range (LLOQ/ULOQ) and judge calibration fit quality beyond R-squared alone.

## When NOT to use

- The data are not a concentration/dilution series with known analyte amounts and replicate levels (CalibraCurve expects calibration experiments from assay development)
- The concentration-intensity relationship is globally non-linear with no usable sub-range, so no linear calibration model can satisfy the PB_av thresholds
- The goal is absolute quantification in new samples rather than assessing/calibrating assay linearity; CalibraCurve builds the calibration basis but does not itself predict concentrations from new data

## Inputs

- Transition-level intensity table from an MRM/PRM dilution series (e.g. Agilent 6490 data from PASSEL accession PASS00653), formatted as a CalibraCurve-compatible concentration-response table with replicate measurements per calibration level

## Outputs

- Final linear range (Cl, Ch) with ordered log of removed calibration levels (linear_range.csv)
- Calibration metrics per weighting scheme: R-squared, PB_av at the lowest level and at specified concentrations (e.g. 0.25 fmol), written to calibration_metrics.csv
- Calibration curve and response factor plots generated by CalibraCurve

## How to apply

Load the concentration-response table (replicate measurements per calibration level) into R and fit linear response-vs-concentration models with CalibraCurve, both unweighted and with 1/x^2 weighting in the regression. Compute the precision-based bias (PB) for each measurement from the residuals, then aggregate to PB_av (mean bias) and PB_cv (bias variability) per level. Iteratively remove boundary levels (lowest Cl, highest Ch, using the documented tie-breaking rules) and refit until PB_av of both retained boundary levels falls below the target threshold TPB_av; the remaining range is the final linear range. When comparing weighted vs unweighted models, never rank fits by R-squared alone: in the MFAP4 WTVFQK case the unweighted model had higher R-squared (~0.994 vs ~0.932) but far worse trueness (PB_av 73.75% vs 14.65% at the lowest level; 72.4% vs 44.6% at 0.25 fmol), so the 1/x^2-weighted model is preferred. Export fitted metrics and the removed-level log to CSV for comparison against published values.

## Evaluation signals

- PB_av of both retained boundary levels (Cl and Ch) is below TPB_av in the final iteration
- linear_range.csv contains the final Cl, Ch values plus the ordered log of removed levels
- Recomputed metrics match the published MFAP4 WTVFQK values: R-squared ~0.994 (unweighted) vs ~0.932 (1/x^2-weighted); PB_av 73.75% vs 14.65% at the lowest level and 72.4% vs 44.6% at 0.25 fmol
- The preferred model is chosen by PB_av trueness, not by maximal R-squared
- Dynamic linear range of the assay falls within the expected 4-5 orders of magnitude typical for SRM/MRM

## Limitations

- R-squared alone is misleading for comparing calibration performance: the unweighted model can attain the higher R-squared while showing substantially worse PB_av trueness at the lowest concentration levels
- Product ion intensities can be reduced at higher analyte concentrations, so the upper end must be bounded via ULOQ determination rather than assumed linear
- The former KNIME-workflow distribution was discontinued; current usage is via the R package or the Nextflow workflow
- The paper provides no discussion-section limitations, so reproducibility caveats beyond the above could not be extracted
